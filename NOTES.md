## Linux-source audit for the fast-path question (2026-11-25, observation only)

- `/home/tom/s21/linux` is a mainline-style kernel tree configured with `CONFIG_DRM=y`, `CONFIG_DRM_SCHED=y`, `CONFIG_DRM_PANFROST=y` and `CONFIG_DRM_EXYNOS2100_SCANOUT=y`. - `/home/tom/s21/kernel` is the Samsung/Exynos S21 vendor source: Exynos2100 DTS has `mali@18500000` with DVFS/thermal data, `drivers/gpu/drm/panfrost/` exists, and `drivers/gpu/arm/bv_r25p1` through `bv_r38p1` are Mali kbase trees with G78 entries. Those kbase trees are the Android/vendor stack; Mesa Panfrost uses the DRM ioctl ABI, not kbase's user ABI.
- The Linux fast path is: Mesa/libdrm Panfrost ioctls -> in-kernel `drm/panfrost` job scheduler, MMU, job slots, flush-ID reduction, interrupts and fences -> GEM/PRIME/dma-buf; display is a KMS/scanout page flip at vblank. There is no Linux DRM scheduler, KMS or real async syncobj in XNU; this is why merely having the Linux sources does not make the iOS path fast.
- Use the Linux tree as a semantics reference and eventually port a minimal DRM/Panfrost ioctl bridge or async submit/fence layer into the XNU SweetMali patch. Keep the Apple framebuffer present path; full KMS is not required. Do not treat this as a drop-in source copy or as a V27/V28 change.
